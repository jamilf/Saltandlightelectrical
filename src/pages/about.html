About

I'm Jamil Flores. I'm looking for an electrical apprenticeship in Western Sydney, and building Salt and Light Electrical in public as I learn the trade.

{{#if person.photo}}{{/if}}

Why the trade

I want to build things that keep doing their job long after I've packed up and gone home. Electrical work is like that. Done properly, it sits behind walls and ceilings for decades, and people rely on it every day without giving it a thought.

Before this I spent about eight years in IT operations and application support, keeping the systems businesses rely on up and running. It taught me good habits. I learnt to write down what I did and why, and to stay calm when something breaks at the worst possible moment.

Most of that work lived on a screen, though. I wanted work I could stand back from and look at, built with my own hands in buildings people use every day.

Every system I looked after also depended on something underneath it that I didn't look after: the power. I'd like to be one of the people who does.

In early 2026 I finished a Certificate II in Electrotechnology, the course that gets people ready for an electrical apprenticeship. Now I'm looking for one, and the IT habits are coming with me.

Starting again as an apprentice after eight years in another career takes some humility. I'll be the newest person on site, learning from people who've been doing this for decades. I think that's the right place to start.

There's a longer goal too. I want to run a business of my own one day, and the trade has a clear path to one. It starts with an apprenticeship. Next comes the NSW certificate that lets an electrician work without supervision, and last of all a contractor licence. Each step has to be earned.

The plan is to open Salt and Light Electrical in {{ site.launchYear }}, once I hold that licence. The road to launch shows each step and where I'm up to.

Why the name

The name comes from Matthew 5:13-16, part of the Sermon on the Mount. Jesus tells his followers that they are the salt of the earth and the light of the world.

In those days, salt was what kept food from going bad. Light lets you see things as they really are. For an electrician, I take that to mean work that lasts, and honesty about what's behind the wall.

The same passage says nobody lights a lamp and then hides it under a bowl. You put it on a stand, where it gives light to everyone in the house. It's hard to think of a better verse for an electrician.

It ends by saying that people should see the good that's done and give the glory to God, not to the person who did it. So the name isn't a boast that I've got everything right. It's a standard I'm asking to be measured against.

Faith and work

I'm a Christian, and I believe ordinary work matters to God. I don't think there's a split between holy jobs and everyday ones. Paul wrote that whatever you do, you should work at it with all your heart, as though you were working for the Lord (Colossians 3:23).

That changes who the work is really for. Work done for God should be done properly even where nobody will ever look, like inside a ceiling or at the back of a switchboard. It's where the promise to do the work once, and do it properly, comes from.

It also shapes how I want to treat people. Jesus said to love your neighbour as yourself. On a job site, I think that looks like safe work and straight answers, even when a straight answer isn't what someone hoped to hear.

One verse suits an apprentice especially well. Jesus said that whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much (Luke 16:10). For the next few years, very little is what I'll be trusted with. I intend to do it well.

My faith matters to me, and it's why the business has this name. You don't need to share it to follow along. Everyone is welcome here, and most of what I write will be about the trade.
